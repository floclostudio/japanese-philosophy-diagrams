Beyond Wabi-sabi — The Sugata Model
The problem was never the fire.
It was the sugata.
姿 — sugata
The beauty the West calls wabi-sabi is the fired bowl. What crossed the ocean was the recipe. What stayed behind was the kiln — the full heat of fire, ego, and contradiction.

Three practitioners. Same fire. Three radically different sugata. Hover each point to trace what happened — and where the shape distorted.
The Sugata Model
器 utsuwa × 姿 sugata
The vessel is how much fire you can hold. The sugata is how that fire appears in the world. It expands in three dimensions: Impact+, Impact−, and Aspiration. A large vessel with a distorted sugata is dangerous. A beautiful sugata with a small vessel is decoration.
Sen no Rikyū
千利休 — 1522–1591
The cone expands beautifully — then distorts. His aspiration peaked at a tea gathering open to all classes. In the same years, his aesthetic monopoly harmed the very world he'd opened. The cone collapses. This is makyo — the zone where ego and service become indistinguishable.
Steve Jobs
1955–2011
The widest cone of the three — billions reached. Yet aspiration plateaus at beauty, never rising to something beyond the self. Impact− never vanishes. Zen reached his products, but not the people beside him. A brilliant shape, stretched sideways, never upward.
Yvon Chouinard
1938–
The only cone where impact− vanishes. He noticed his pitons were scarring the rock, and chose to stop selling them — his most profitable product. From that moment, the lower edge contracts while aspiration climbs. The cone sheds its shadow and rises. This is shūren — cultivation, not suppression.
